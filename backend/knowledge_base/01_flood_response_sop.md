# Flood Response Standard Operating Procedure

## 1. Activation thresholds

Flood response escalates through defined trigger points rather than
discretionary judgement:

- **Watch** — Upstream rainfall exceeds 64.5 mm in 24 hours, or a gauge crosses
  its warning level with a rising trend.
- **Alert** — A gauge is within 1.0 m of its danger level with a sustained rate
  of rise, or an upstream reservoir begins controlled spill.
- **Emergency** — A gauge crosses its danger level, or inundation of inhabited
  areas is confirmed by field report or imagery.

The rate of rise matters more than the absolute level. A river 1.5 m below
danger but climbing 0.25 m/hour gives roughly six hours of usable lead time;
the same level static gives days. Evacuation decisions must be driven by
projected time-to-danger, not current level.

## 2. Dam and reservoir coordination

When an upstream reservoir initiates spill, downstream authorities must be
notified before release, not concurrently. Release schedules should be
published with expected downstream arrival times per gauge station. Sudden
unannounced release is a recurring cause of avoidable casualties and must be
treated as a coordination failure, not an act of nature.

## 3. Evacuation sequencing

Evacuate in this order when capacity is constrained:

1. Persons in single-storey structures within the projected inundation zone.
2. Persons with reduced mobility — elderly, disabled, hospitalised, pregnant.
3. Households with infants and young children.
4. Livestock and moveable assets, only once human evacuation is complete.

Do not begin evacuation of low-risk zones before high-risk zones are cleared,
even where low-risk zones are easier to reach. Ease of access is not a
prioritisation criterion.

## 4. Rescue operations

- Boat operations require a minimum of two crew plus one trained swimmer.
- Night-time boat rescue in flowing water should be avoided unless life is
  under immediate threat; the casualty rate among responders rises sharply.
- Never drive or wade through moving water of unknown depth. Thirty centimetres
  of moving water is sufficient to sweep a passenger vehicle.
- Electrical supply to inundated areas must be isolated before rescue teams
  enter. Confirm isolation with the distribution utility, do not assume it.

## 5. Shelter activation

Relief camps must be activated at the Alert stage, not the Emergency stage.
A camp opened after inundation begins cannot be reached by the population it
was intended to serve. Camps must be verified as outside the projected
inundation zone; designating a flood-prone school as a shelter is a common and
serious planning error.

## 6. Information gaps to close first

The following unknowns most degrade flood decision quality and should be
prioritised for active collection:

- Current inundation extent, confirmed by imagery rather than inference.
- Status of road and bridge access to each affected settlement.
- Number of persons remaining in the inundation zone, by settlement.
- Reservoir release schedule for the next 24 hours.
- Which designated shelters are themselves flooded or unreachable.
